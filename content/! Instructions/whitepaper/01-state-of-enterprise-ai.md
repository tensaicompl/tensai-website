### 1. The Adoption-Impact Paradox: 88% Adopt, 6% Win

**Source:** McKinsey Global AI Survey 2025 — "The State of AI: Agents, Innovation, and Transformation" (November 2025)
**URL:** https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/quantumblack/our-insights/the-state-of-ai
**PDF:** https://www.mckinsey.com/~/media/mckinsey/business%20functions/quantumblack/our%20insights/the%20state%20of%20ai/november%202025/the-state-of-ai-2025-agents-innovation_cmyk-v1.pdf
**Methodology:** 1,993 participants across 105 nations, June-July 2025
**Key data points:**
- 88% of organisations use AI in at least one business function (up from 78% a year ago)
- Two-thirds use AI in multiple functions; half deploy across 3+ functions
- Nearly two-thirds have NOT yet begun scaling AI across the enterprise — still experimenting or running pilots
- Only 39% attribute any EBIT impact to AI; among those, most report <5% of EBIT attributable to AI
- Only 6% of respondents qualify as "AI high performers" (>5% EBIT from AI + significant value delivered)
- 62% of organisations are at least experimenting with AI agents; 23% are scaling agentic AI
- 64% say AI is enabling their innovation
- High performers are 3x more likely to have strong senior leadership ownership
- High performers are 3.6x more likely to target transformative change (not just efficiency)
- 75% of high performers are scaling or have scaled AI, vs. 33% of others
- >33% of high performers commit >20% of digital budgets to AI

**Relevance to whitepaper:** This is the definitive framing stat — nearly universal adoption masks a massive value-creation gap, establishing the core thesis that adoption without systematic scaling is wasted investment.

### 2. The 95% Pilot Failure Rate

**Source:** MIT NANDA Initiative — "The GenAI Divide: State of AI in Business 2025" (August 2025)
**URL:** https://fortune.com/2025/08/18/mit-report-95-percent-generative-ai-pilots-at-companies-failing-cfo/
**Methodology:** 150 leader interviews, 350 employee surveys, analysis of 300 public AI deployments
**Key data points:**
- ~95% of AI pilot programmes fail to achieve rapid revenue acceleration; vast majority stall with little to no measurable P&L impact
- Only ~5% of AI pilots achieve rapid revenue acceleration
- More than half of GenAI budgets go to sales and marketing tools, yet the biggest ROI is in back-office automation (eliminating BPO, cutting agency costs, streamlining operations)
- Purchasing AI from specialised vendors succeeds ~67% of the time; internal builds succeed only ~22% (one-third as often)
- Generic tools (e.g., ChatGPT) excel for individuals but stall in enterprise use — they don't learn from or adapt to workflows
- Share of enterprises abandoning most AI initiatives jumped from 17% (2024) to 42% (2025)

**Relevance to whitepaper:** Provides the shocking headline failure rate and identifies root causes (generic tools, budget misallocation, lack of workflow integration) that TensAI's approach directly addresses.

### 3. RAND Corporation: 80% Deliver No Business Value

**Source:** RAND Corporation — AI Project Failure Analysis (2025)
**URL:** https://www.rand.org/pubs/presentations/PTA2680-1.html
**Secondary:** https://www.pertamapartners.com/insights/ai-project-failure-statistics-2026
**Methodology:** Meta-analysis of 65 documented enterprise AI initiatives, 2022-2025
**Key data points:**
- 80.3% of AI projects fail to deliver intended business value (2x the failure rate of non-AI IT projects)
- Breakdown: 33.8% abandoned before reaching production, 28.4% reach completion but fail to deliver expected value, 18.1% deliver some value but cannot justify costs
- In 2025, global enterprises invested $684B in AI initiatives; by year-end, >$547B had failed to deliver intended business value
- Success factors in every successful case: (1) data domain cleaned before project start, (2) decision-making structure clear, (3) use case scoped tightly enough to prevent drift

**Relevance to whitepaper:** Complements MIT's 95% figure with a different methodology and more granular failure-mode breakdown, strengthening the case for structured implementation approaches.

### 4. The Widening AI Value Gap: 5% Lead, 60% Lag

**Source:** BCG — "The Widening AI Value Gap: Build for the Future" (September 2025)
**URL:** https://www.bcg.com/publications/2025/are-you-generating-value-from-ai-the-widening-gap
**Press release:** https://www.bcg.com/press/30september2025-ai-leaders-outpace-laggards-revenue-growth-cost-savings
**Methodology:** 1,250 senior executives and AI decision makers across 9 industries, 25+ sectors, assessed across 41 foundational capabilities
**Key data points:**
- 5% "future-built" — systematically building cutting-edge AI capabilities, consistently generating substantial value
- 35% "scalers" — scaling up, beginning to generate value, but admit they could move faster
- 60% "laggards" — reporting minimal revenue and cost gains despite investment
- Future-built companies achieve: 5x the revenue increases, 3x the cost reductions, 1.7x revenue growth, 3.6x three-year TSR, 1.6x EBIT margin vs. laggards
- Future-built plan to spend 26% more on IT (almost a full percentage point of revenue) and dedicate up to 64% more of IT budget to AI
- AI agents account for 17% of total AI value in 2025, expected to reach 29% by 2028
- A third of future-built companies use agents, vs. 12% of scalers, vs. nearly 0% of laggards

**Relevance to whitepaper:** Provides the three-tier segmentation framework and demonstrates that the gap is widening, not closing — creating urgency for enterprises in the middle 35% who risk falling further behind.

### 8. Deloitte: The Untapped Edge — Production Scaling Gap

**Source:** Deloitte AI Institute — "The State of AI in the Enterprise: The Untapped Edge" (2026 edition)
**URL:** https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/about/press-room/state-of-ai-report-2026.html
**Report page:** https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/what-we-do/capabilities/applied-artificial-intelligence/content/state-of-ai-in-the-enterprise.html
**Methodology:** 3,235 leaders across 24 countries, 6 industries, August-September 2025
**Key data points:**
- Worker access to AI rose by 50% in 2025 (from <40% to ~60% of workers with sanctioned tools)
- Only 25% of organisations have converted 40%+ of pilots into production systems (but 54% expect to reach that level in 3-6 months)
- 66% report productivity and efficiency gains from enterprise AI
- Only 20% are currently growing revenue through AI; 74% hope to in the future
- 34% using AI to deeply transform (new products, reinvented processes, new business models)
- 30% redesigning key processes around AI
- 37% using AI at surface level with little/no change to existing processes
- Insufficient worker skills identified as the biggest barrier to AI workflow integration
- Fewer than 60% of employees with AI access regularly use the tools

**Relevance to whitepaper:** The 25% production conversion rate and the 20% vs. 74% revenue gap (actual vs. aspirational) demonstrate that most enterprises are stuck between adoption and value extraction.

## Gaps Identified

1. **Sector-specific failure rates**: While we have aggregate failure rates (80-95%), there is limited publicly available data on how failure rates vary by industry (financial services vs. manufacturing vs. healthcare). BCG's 41-capability framework hints at this but doesn't publish sector-specific success rates.

2. **Cost of failed AI pilots**: RAND provides a macro estimate ($547B wasted in 2025), but there is no authoritative per-project or per-company cost-of-failure analysis.

3. **Mid-market specific data**: Most surveys focus on large enterprises (500+ employees). Data on AI adoption and success rates for mid-market companies (100-500 employees) is thin.

4. **Time-to-value metrics**: No major survey tracks how long it takes enterprises to move from pilot to production value, or what the typical timeline looks like.

5. **AI team composition and size**: Limited data on how many AI specialists enterprises employ relative to their AI ambitions.

6. **Technical debt from AI**: No major survey quantifies the technical debt being accumulated from rapid, unstructured AI deployments.

7. **EU AI Act impact on adoption**: While the Act is frequently mentioned as a concern, no authoritative study measures its actual impact on adoption rates or timelines.

8. **Open-source vs. proprietary model adoption in enterprise**: Limited enterprise-specific data on the DeepSeek/open-source model impact on enterprise strategies.

9. **Year-over-year success rate trends**: While adoption rates are tracked YoY, success/failure rates are not consistently tracked with the same methodology across years, making trend analysis difficult.

**Sources:** McKinsey 6% high performers, ServiceNow maturity decline, Deloitte 25% production conversion, Gartner Stage 2-to-3 analysis

**Key argument:** The top performers share five characteristics: (1) transformative ambition beyond efficiency, (2) committed senior leadership, (3) workflow redesign (not just tool deployment), (4) substantial and sustained investment, (5) rigorous measurement and governance.
